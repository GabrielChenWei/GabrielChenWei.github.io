    Question 006 of 68

    (QID: cannot be determined) ABC Ltd. applies for a financial adviser's licence for advising clients directly on futures contracts and OTC currency derivatives. Its NAV at the end of the previous year was $10 million. To be considered eligible, ABC:

    Must take a standalone non-hybrid professional indemnity insurance policy (PII) cover with the limit of indemnity covered of minimum $2 million. Need not have a PII because its NAV at the end of the preceding year was $10 million. May take a group PII cover with minimum coverage of $2.5 million and a maximum deductible of $2 million. Must take a group hybrid PII but the deductible of the PII must not be greater than $2 million.

    Show AnswerExplanation: ABC must have in force a standalone non-hybrid PII under which the limit of indemnity covered should be minimum $500,000 and under which the deductible allowed must not be more than 20% of its NAV at the end of its immediately preceding financial year. Alternatively, it may have a group PII policy with a cover of minimum 5 times that required for a standalone policy ($5 x $500,000 = $2.5 million). The deductible should be maximum 20% of the NAV as above (= $10 million x 20% = $2 million). If the deductible is higher, ABC must give an undertaking from its parent company to cover the excess in the event of a claim. Similar undertaking may work if ABC goes for a group hybrid PII. Therefore, the 20% deductible limit is NOT a must.

    Question 008 of 68

    (QID: 5227e47806) An FA is required to disclose, in writing, the remuneration that it has received under the FAA, except:

    soft dollar commissions from product providers. trailer commissions from product providers. details of all fees charged to the client. details of all fees charged for non-financial advisory activities.

    Show AnswerExplanation: Except for fees charged for non-financial activities, an FA should disclose, in writing, all remuneration (include any commissions, fees, and other benefits) that it (will) receive for making any recommendation in respect of an investment product, or executing a purchase or sale contract relating to an investment product on the client's behalf. If an FA is charging a fee, it should disclose the details of the charges at the outset. When remuneration is not quantifiable, an FA should furnish its client with a description of how it will be remunerated. If its precise rate or value is not known in advance, the FA should provide a reasonable estimate.

    Question 009 of 68

    (QID: 5227e47808) Which of the following is NOT an investment product defined in the FAA?

    Spot foreign exchange contracts for leveraged forex trading A life policy Shares of an SGX-listed company Term deposits

    Show AnswerExplanation: FAA defines investment products as any capital market product as defined in Section 2(1) of the Securities and Futures Act, spot foreign exchange contracts other than for the purposes of leveraged foreign exchange trading, any life policy, or any other products as may be prescribed. Deposit-taking products (e.g., fixed/term deposits) offered by banks are EXCLUDED as such products are at the low end of the risk spectrum and are well understood. Capital markets products include any securities, units in a CIS, derivatives contracts, and spot foreign exchange contracts for leveraged foreign exchange trading. The term securities includes shares or units in business trusts.
