The base NAV is $1. The management fee is 3% and the performance fee is 8%. In years 1, 2, 3, & 4, the fund's returns are 4%, -6%, 5% and, 4%, while the benchmark's returns are 6%, -5%, 3%, and 3%.     Question 035 of 130

    (QID: 5227e47737) XYZ Fund uses the High Water Mark (HWM) method for calculating its performance fees. The initial NAV of the fund is $100 and John (an investor) buys 1,000 units. The NAV (bid price) at the end of year 1, 2, & 3 is $140, $120, & $160. The performance fee is 10%. John sells the 1,000 units at the end of 3 years. Based on this data (ignoring other charges, and presuming that base fee and performance fee are calculated based on year-end NAV per unit):

    Performance fee is payable at the end of year 2. The total performance fee payable is $12,000. The net profit made by John is $54,000. Had John sold the units at the end of the 1st year, his ROI would have been 40%.

    Show AnswerExplanation: The performance fee payable at the end of the first year is ($140 - $100) * 1,000 * 10% = $4,000. So, the ROI at the end of the first year is ($40,000 - $4,000) / $100,000 = 36%. No performance fee is payable at the end of year 2 because the NAV is below the HWM of $140. At the end of year 3, the performance fee payable is ($160 - $140) * 1,000 * 10% = $2,000. Therefore, at the end of year 3, the total performance fee paid is $4,000 + $2,000 = $6,000. The selling price of the units is $160, so the net profit is ($160 - $100) * 1000 - $6,000 = $54,000.
