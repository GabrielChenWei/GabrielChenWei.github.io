    Question 008 of 96

    (QID: 5227e58336) The finance manager of Company X is a personal client of yours. She informs you to sell a substantial portion of her personal holdings of the company's stock. Upon receiving this order, you chatted with a colleague, Coco, over lunch break about it. Coco, who happens to own some Company X stock, sold all her holdings. Which market misconduct are you primarily in breach of?

    Dissemination of Information about Illegal Transactions Breach of Confidentiality Breach of Fiduciary Duty Insider Trading

    Show AnswerExplanation: The finance manager (an insider) possesses material non-public information about the company's upcoming financial performance announcement. By ordering a large sale through the trader, this constitutes potential insider trading by the manager. More critically, the trader – upon receiving and understanding the order's implications – tips (passes) this confidential, material information to a colleague (a tippee), who then trades on it by selling the stock. This is classic insider trading under securities laws, specifically involving tipping liability, where both the tipper (trader) and tippee (colleague) can be liable when the information is used for trading in breach of a duty of trust and confidence. While a breach of confidentiality occurs as part of the process, the overarching and specific violation in the securities context is insider trading.
